This policy governs my future use of generative AI in doctoral work. Any retrospective disclosure must describe actual past use truthfully rather than applying this policy retroactively to hide or reclassify it.

## 1. GREEN zone — permitted with acknowledgement

I may use AI tools for:

- brainstorming possible topics or questions;
- outlining documents before I write them;
- grammar, spelling, clarity, and translation of text I drafted;
- code scaffolding, debugging suggestions, and test-case ideas;
- literature discovery, keyword expansion, and reference-management support;
- formatting Markdown, tables, and repository structure.

Requirements:

1. I verify every output.
2. I do not cite the AI as a factual source.
3. I acknowledge material assistance in a footnote or disclosure statement.
4. I do not include confidential operational information in prompts.

## 2. AMBER zone — prior approval, disclosure, and prompt log

I may use AI for the following only with prior written supervisor approval:

- paragraph-level drafting of non-core administrative sections;
- data-cleaning or documentation scripts;
- language polishing of arguments I already developed;
- summaries of papers I personally read and checked;
- generation of synthetic examples clearly labelled as synthetic.

For each AMBER use:

- approver: supervisor or instructor;
- prompt-log location: private repository or encrypted project record;
- retention: at least five years after thesis completion, subject to institutional policy;
- inspection: supervisor, committee, or integrity authority on request;
- disclosure: tool, task, date, and verification procedure.

AMBER is a paper trail, not a loophole. If the approval and log cannot be produced, the use must not be represented as compliant.

## 3. RED line — prohibited

I will not use AI to produce or replace:

- core scientific analysis;
- final interpretation of results;
- methodology decisions presented as my own;
- discussion and conclusions;
- the thesis or paper abstract;
- fabricated or altered data;
- citations I have not verified;
- peer reviews that I sign;
- examination, midterm, final-presentation, or defense responses;
- confidential-data analysis through an unapproved external service.

I will not conceal AI use, present synthetic results as real, or use AI-generated figures without provenance and expert validation.

## 4. Verification rules

- Every citation must be checked against the publisher or primary record.
- Every code contribution must be reviewed and tested.
- Every quantitative claim must be reproducible from authorized data and code.
- Every figure must have provenance.
- Every AI-generated suggestion is treated as an unverified draft.
